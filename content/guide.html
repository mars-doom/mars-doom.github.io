Maker guide

How to get your project onto its page: from your phone in a few minutes, then from a computer and an AI agent when you want more.

How it fits together

Your project lives in its own GitHub repo, github.com/mars-doom/your-project. Everything you add there shows up in two places a few minutes later:

  • Your docs site: mars-doom.github.io/your-project/
  • The hub: your card and pages on mars-doom.github.io, refreshed every night.

Your repo has four pages to fill in:

PageWhat goes in it
SummaryYour best photo and a few sentences: what it is, who it's for.
LogShort dated entries as you go. Photos, notes, what broke.
DesignHow it works, a diagram, your parts list and design files.
Build itSteps for someone who wants to make their own.

Done looks like: a photo and a few sentences, a log with a start and an end, a diagram with a parts list, and the main steps to build one.

1. On your phone: Pages CMS

Pages CMS is a website that edits your repo like a normal app: typing, photos, Save. No installs, no Git.

Get access (once)

  1. Make a GitHub account at github.com/signup if you don't have one.
  2. Send your GitHub username to a DooM lead. They'll add you to your project repo.
  3. Accept the invite from the email GitHub sends you.

Open your project

  1. Go to app.pagescms.org and tap Sign in with GitHub.
  2. Pick mars-doom, then your project.
  3. Add it to your home screen so it opens like an app: iPhone: Share → Add to Home Screen. Android: ⋮ → Add to Home screen.

If it asks to approve Pages CMS

The first time anyone in mars-doom uses it, GitHub may ask for the Pages CMS app to be approved for the organization. Ask a DooM lead: an org owner approves it once, for everyone.

What's in the menu

  • Summary, Log, Design, Build it: your four pages.
  • Parts list: a table. Only item and qty are required. In cost_each type a number, or a word like salvaged or free. Use section to group parts to match your diagram.
  • Media → Photos and Media → Files: everything you've uploaded.
  • Setup: site settings. Leave these alone unless you know what you're changing.

Write and save

  1. Tap a page and type. The toolbar does bold, lists and headings.
  2. Tap Save. Your docs site updates a minute or two later.

Two things to leave alone

Page title: never leave it empty, or Pages CMS can't reopen the page.
Hidden prompts: some pages have grey questions to get you going. They never show on your site, so you can leave them or delete them.

Writing a log entry

Put new entries at the top, under a heading with the date and a few words:

### 2026-11-02 · First water test

Then a few lines and a photo or two. Write it the same day: it's very hard to remember later, and these notes become your Build it page at the end.

2. Photos

Phone photos are too big for the website (3–8 MB each). Shrink them first. Your originals stay full size on your phone.

  1. Open Shrink photos on your phone. Add it to your home screen too.
  2. Tap Choose photos, pick as many as you like, then Save all. Small copies (2000 px, about 300 KB) land in your camera roll next to the originals.
  3. In Pages CMS, tap the image button in the toolbar where you want the photo, and pick the small copy.

Shrinking also removes the photo's location, so you don't publish where you took it.

Photos that work

  • Summary photo: the whole thing, in good light, held sideways (landscape). It's also your card on the hub.
  • One photo per build step on Build it. A photo of each step is a gift to whoever tries this next.
  • Diagrams: a sketch on paper with the parts labelled, photographed, is perfect.
  • Describe what each photo shows. In the editor that's the image's alt text.

3. Videos

Don't upload video files to your repo. They're too big: anything over 50 MB is rejected, and smaller ones still slow the site down.

  1. Upload to YouTube (Unlisted is fine) or Vimeo.
  2. Paste the link on its own line on your page, with a sentence saying what it shows.
  3. Add a still photo from the video next to the link, so the page still makes sense if the video goes away.

4. Design files

CAD, drawings, cut files, code and data go in the files folder. Everything there is listed on your Design page automatically, with download links.

  • From your phone: Pages CMS → Media → Files → upload.
  • From a computer: put them in files/ in your repo (see below).

Use folders so people can find things:

files/
  cad/           models: your source file + STEP
  drawings/      PDFs, DXFs, sketches
  print/         STL / 3MF for 3D printing
  cut/           DXF / SVG for laser, CNC, plasma
  code/          firmware, scripts (with a README)
  data/          test results, spreadsheets

Share two versions of each design: the file you worked in, so others can edit it (Fusion, FreeCAD, SolidWorks, KiCad…), and one anyone can open (STEP, DXF, STL, PDF).

Name files plainly: frame-v2.step, not Untitled (3) FINAL.f3d.

Size: files over 5 MB get a warning, files over 50 MB are rejected. Put big things (scans, large models, datasets) on Zenodo or a shared drive and link to them from your Design page.

5. On a computer: GitHub Desktop

A computer is better for long writing, lots of files, and working with an AI agent. GitHub Desktop is the easiest way to get your repo onto it.

Set up (once)

  1. Install GitHub Desktop and sign in with your GitHub account.
  2. File → Clone repository, find mars-doom/your-project, choose a folder (e.g. Documents/GitHub), and click Clone.
  3. Install a text editor if you don't have one. VS Code is free and shows a preview of your pages.

Where things are

In your folderWhat it is
docs/index.mdSummary page
docs/log.mdLog
docs/design.mdDesign
docs/build-it.mdBuild it
docs/images/Photos for your pages
parts.csvParts list (opens in any spreadsheet app)
files/Design files
mkdocs.ymlSite settings and page menu

Pages are Markdown: plain text where # Heading makes a heading, - item makes a list, and ![what it shows](images/photo.jpg) adds a photo.

Every time you work

  1. Pull first. In GitHub Desktop click Fetch origin, then Pull origin if it offers. This brings in anything you saved from your phone. Skipping it is the most common way to get stuck.
  2. Edit files, add photos to docs/images/ (shrink them first), add design files to files/.
  3. In GitHub Desktop, check the list of changes, write a short summary (e.g. "Add frame build steps"), click Commit to main, then Push origin.

Your site updates a couple of minutes after you push.

Preview before you push (optional)

With Python installed, run pip install -r requirements.txt once in your repo folder, then mkdocs serve, and open http://localhost:8000. It refreshes as you edit.

6. With your AI agent

Once your repo is on your computer, an AI coding agent (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and the like) can do the tidy-up work: turning notes into log entries, drafting build steps, filling in the parts list.

Point it at your repo

  1. Open your project folder in the agent (the folder you cloned above).
  2. Start with context: 
    This is my Mars College Dept of Makers project repo. Read README.md,
mkdocs.yml and the pages in docs/ so you know how it's laid out.
The site is built with MkDocs; parts.csv feeds the parts list.
  3. Then ask for something specific.

Things to ask it

  • "Here are my notes from today: … Turn them into a log entry at the top of docs/log.md, dated today."
  • "Using my log and the photos in docs/images/, draft the steps for docs/build-it.md. Put a photo with each step and mark anything you're unsure of with TODO."
  • "Add these parts to parts.csv, in the Frame section: …"
  • "Write a short README for files/code/ explaining how to flash this firmware."
  • "Run mkdocs build --strict and fix any broken links or images."
  • "Shrink any photos in docs/images/ that are over 1 MB" (it can run tools/resize_photos.py).

Ground rules

  • Check its work before you push. GitHub Desktop shows every line it changed. It's your project and your name on it.
  • Facts come from you. Measurements, costs, what actually happened: an agent will happily invent plausible ones.
  • Keep it in your lane: pages in docs/, parts.csv and files/. Don't let it change .github/, tools/, .pages.yml or rename pages unless you mean to: the site and Pages CMS depend on them.
  • Pull before it starts, so it isn't working on an old copy.
  • No secrets. Never put passwords, API keys or Wi-Fi details in your repo; it's public.

Stuck?

  • Page won't open in Pages CMS: its title is probably empty. Fix it from a computer, or ask a DooM lead.
  • Site didn't update: on GitHub, open your repo's Actions tab. A red ✗ means the build failed; the log usually names the file.
  • GitHub Desktop says your branch is behind or has conflicts: you edited in two places at once. Ask for help before forcing anything.
  • Anything else: ask a DooM lead.
